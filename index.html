The problem

Apps quietly start collecting more. You never get told.

Every app on the App Store has a "privacy nutrition label" that says what it collects. Apps can update those labels whenever they want. One day Instagram doesn't track your precise location. The next morning it does, and nobody emails you about it. privacytracker checks every day and lets you know when something changes.

  • Spots when an app starts collecting something new
  • Flags when something gets more invasive (like location going from rough to exact)
  • Notices when an app drops a permission too, which is sometimes good news

Plain-English summaries

Privacy policies, written for humans.

Privacy policies are written for lawyers, so nobody reads them. privacytracker rewrites every one in plain English, answering the same five questions for every app, so you can actually compare them.

  • What data does it collect, and how much?
  • Does it share with advertisers?
  • Who else gets your data?
  • How long do they keep it?
  • Can you ask them to delete it?

More inside

Made for everyday use.

privacytracker runs in the background and only speaks up when something actually changes.

Add apps the easy way

Just type names (Instagram, TikTok, your bank) or plug in your iPhone and load every app at once. No App Store password needed.

Checks while you sleep

Pick daily or weekly and forget about it. privacytracker checks on its own and only nudges you when something actually changes.

No spam, no nags

You'll only see a notification when an app you actually track does something new. There are no marketing emails because there's no email signup at all.

Stays on your computer

privacytracker doesn't send anything to us. There's no "us." Your list of apps and the changes it spots stay on your Mac.

Open-source & free

Anyone can read the code that powers privacytracker. Free forever, with no premium tier, upgrade prompts, or surprise charges.

Take your data with you

Export everything to a regular spreadsheet whenever you want. Share with a friend, save it for your records, or just take a look in Excel. There's a proper backup too, a single file with everything in it, so you can put it all back if you change Macs.

English and 中文

The whole app is translated into Simplified Chinese, including every screen and every menu. Switch language in Settings and it stays that way. More languages are on the way.

Checking a child's phone

Tell privacytracker you're looking after a child's device and it changes what it shows you. Age ratings get checked alongside the privacy labels, and there's a plain-English walkthrough of Apple's parental controls.

Readable for everyone

There's an accessibility mode where colour is never the only signal, so every warning carries a shape and a word too. Text contrast meets WCAG AA throughout, and automated checks run on the main screens with every change so it doesn't regress.

Common questions

The things people usually ask.

If you've got a question that isn't here, the source code is open and the maintainers read every issue on GitHub.

Is privacytracker really free?

Yes. It's free and open-source under the Apache 2.0 licence. There are no accounts, subscriptions, or upsells. The full source is on GitHub for anyone to read or fork.

Does privacytracker need access to my iPhone?

No. It only reads the public App Store pages for apps you tell it about, which is exactly what you'd see if you opened the App Store yourself. Nothing on your phone is touched, paired, or copied across.

Where does my data go?

Onto your computer, and nowhere else by default. privacytracker keeps a small local database in its own folder. There's no cloud account, no analytics, and no telemetry phoning home.

If you'd like the full breakdown of every network call the app makes, see the privacy disclosure.

What's different from looking at the App Store myself?

The App Store only shows you what an app says today. privacytracker remembers what every app said yesterday, last month, and last year, and tells you when something changes. It also reads each developer's privacy policy and translates the legalese into plain English.

Do I have to be technical to use it?

Not at all. The recommended install is a single file: double-click, drag it into Applications, you're done. The Terminal-style options on the install page are there for people who prefer them. They're not required.

Does it work on Windows or Linux?

The desktop app is Mac-only for now. If you're on Windows or Linux, the "Run it yourself" option lets you spin up the same app in Docker, which works on any operating system and takes about a minute of setup.

Will it watch Android apps too?

Not yet. iOS first because Apple's privacy labels are uniquely structured, and Google Play doesn't publish the same kind of disclosure. We'd love to add Android once that data is reliably available in a comparable form.

Can I help a parent or partner without taking over their phone?

Yes. That's what the Help a friend section is about. privacytracker runs on your computer; your friend just tells you which apps they use and you keep an eye on the labels for them. There's no remote access, no shared account, and nothing to set up on their phone.
